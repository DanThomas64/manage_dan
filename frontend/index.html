How this tab works
Two kinds of chart: Net Worth (one line — every account added together) and one chart per account below it, so any single account's own trend stays readable regardless of scale. A liability's chart already assumes its minimum payment is being paid, for real — set one up as a real recurring transfer on the Recurring tab (not here) and every chart, real or preview, reflects it automatically from then on; it uses that account's interest rate too, compounding for real, not just a flat forecast. To try a hypothetical extra payment (or a raise, a new subscription, anything else), check a Saved Scenario below — its items fold into every chart's dashed preview line at once, only ever excluding the real payment they're linked to via "Replaces," never silently stacking with it. Budget Caps at the bottom is a separate concern (spending limits, not balance trends) — unless you check "Include in projection" on an allocation, in which case it joins the same preview too.
Saved Scenarios (check to apply to the charts below)
A scenario is a named, saved "what if" — e.g. a raise, a new subscription, an extra debt payment. Create one, open its Items list, and add income/expense lines by hand, or hit Import Recurring to copy every real recurring item in as a starting point you can then edit freely (editing a scenario item never touches the real one). Each item's "Replaces" field decides whether it stands in for a real payment (excluded automatically so it never stacks — set for you on import, or pick one by hand) or is a genuinely new payment (nothing excluded). Checking a scenario's box folds its items into every chart below; unchecking removes them again. You can check multiple scenarios at once to combine them.
Net Worth (12 months)
Every account's own chart below, added together into one line — assets plus liabilities, with liabilities negative, so more debt lowers this. Liability accounts contribute their interest-aware payoff projection here too, not a flat forecast, so this reflects real compounding debt. The dashed preview line (when present) is the same sum with every checked scenario applied to whichever accounts they target.
Accounts
One chart per account, always shown — asset accounts use the plain forecast (real transactions plus real recurring items/transfers); liability accounts use the interest-aware payoff math instead (that account's rate, plus real recurring items/transfers against it — a recurring transfer into it already pays it down, out of it already makes it worse, exactly like a scheduled expense/income item). Every card's breakdown line states exactly what's counted. Both react to scenarios checked above.
Monthly Budget Caps
A category's overall budget cap is the sum of its allocations — split it across as many accounts as you like (e.g. part of "Stupid" on a credit card, part on checking), compared against this month's actual Spending-tab totals plus any recurring expense items you've tagged with that category on the Recurring tab (their amounts normalized to a monthly equivalent and shown as "+ $X/mo recurring" — a subscription counts against your budget even before it's actually charged again). Each allocation has its own "Include in projection" checkbox to fold it into the charts above as an ongoing monthly expense — off by default, so adding an allocation alone never changes anything else.
